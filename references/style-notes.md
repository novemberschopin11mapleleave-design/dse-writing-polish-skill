# Style Notes

## Formality quick guide

| Genre | Prefer | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Formal letter/report | precise, courteous, objective | slang, unnecessary contractions, exaggerated praise |
| Proposal | constructive, logical, practical | vague promises, unsupported claims |
| Speech | direct, rhythmic, audience-aware | bureaucratic phrasing |
| Article/blog | lively, relevant, reader-friendly | unrelated jokes or heavy slang |
| Review | evaluative, specific, balanced | unsupported superlatives |
| Leaflet | clear, inviting, action-oriented | long analytical paragraphs |

## Replacement principles

- Replace **a lot of** only when the target audience and quantity require it. **a large number of** usually suits countable nouns; **a considerable amount of** suits some uncountable nouns.
- Replace **good** with a functional noun or adjective only when the context supports it: **effective**, **well-designed**, **successful**, or **high-quality**.
- Replace **bad** with a word that identifies the problem: **poor**, **ineffective**, **unacceptable**, **damaging**, or **harmful**.
- Replace **interesting** based on subject: **thought-provoking** for ideas, **engaging** for content or experiences, **memorable** for an event, and **unusual** for an unexpected feature.
- Prefer a precise verb over an adverb-heavy construction. For example, **move quickly** may become **hurry** only if the context supports urgency.

## Tone checks

Before delivering a rewrite, ask:

- Is the student addressing the right reader?
- Has confidence been overstated or softened?
- Does the text sound warm or merely casual?
- Are persuasive claims supported by existing evidence?
- Is criticism firm but respectful?
- Could the rewrite be naturally spoken or read in the intended setting?
